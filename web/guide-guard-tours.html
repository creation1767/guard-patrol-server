How to run guard tours without paper logs

Put a QR label at each checkpoint, have officers scan it on every round, and the time-stamped scans become your proof of patrol. Most sites can switch over in one afternoon.

Why paper patrol logs fail

  • Entries are filled in after the fact, so times are guesses.
  • Logs get lost, wet or unreadable, and they never reach the office until the end of the week.
  • When a client asks "did anyone check the garage at 2 a.m.?", you can't prove it.

Set up QR guard tours in five steps

  1. Walk the site and pick checkpoints. Choose the places that matter: doors that must be locked, the loading dock, garage levels, the roof access, the perimeter fence.
  2. Write one instruction per checkpoint. For example "Roll-up door closed and padlocked after 19:00." Officers see it the moment they scan.
  3. Print and place the QR labels. Use weatherproof labels outdoors and mount them where an officer has to physically be to scan them.
  4. Set how many rounds per shift. A round is complete when every checkpoint has been scanned once.
  5. Train officers in two minutes. Tap Scan checkpoint, point the camera at the label, choose All clear or Issue found, and add a note if something is wrong.

What happens when an officer finds a problem

Marking Issue found at a checkpoint should lead straight into an incident report with the location already filled in. The supervisor gets it immediately instead of reading about it the next morning.

Turn scans into proof for your clients

Every scan carries the officer, the checkpoint and the exact time. Share those records with the property through a client portal, or export them to Excel for a monthly report. Clients who can see the patrols are clients who renew.
