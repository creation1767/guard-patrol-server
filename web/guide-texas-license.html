Texas Level 2 vs Level 3 vs Level 4 security licenses

A Level 2 license covers unarmed security work after a 6-hour course. Level 3 is for armed (commissioned) officers and takes a 45-hour course plus firearm qualification. Level 4 adds personal protection with a 15-hour course.

Level 2: unarmed officers

Level 2 is the "guard card" most officers start with. It covers unarmed work such as access control, front desks, patrols, construction sites and events. Officers take a 6-hour course from a licensed Texas training school, pass the test, and apply through the DPS TOPS system with fingerprints.

Level 3: armed (commissioned) officers

Level 3 lets an officer carry a firearm on the job while working for a licensed company. The Level 3 course is at least 45 hours and includes a firearm proficiency course of fire. Level 3 officers must complete 6 hours of continuing education to renew.

Level 4: personal protection officers

Level 4 is for executive protection and bodyguard work. It requires a minimum 15-hour course and test on top of a Level 3 commission, and the same 6 hours of continuing education.

What this means for scheduling

Mixed teams need the right officer on the right post. Keep armed and unarmed posts clear in your post orders, schedule by license level, and keep a record of who patrolled where and when. That record is what clients and insurers ask for after an incident.
